\section{Existence-Proof Experiment: Does the Trigger+Repair Mechanism Ever Activate?}
\label{sec:existence-proof}

Experiments 1, 3, and 4 all show SAR-CRP tying Static exactly (0/20
nonzero seed pairs) on every uncertainty level, layout, and confidence
level tested. That is a benchmark-calibration fact (\S20 SC4: mean event
impact 0.090, well under $\theta_{\text{impact}}=0.30$), not proof the
trigger+repair mechanism itself never works. This experiment settles the
question directly with two deliberately engineered, deterministic
high-impact events (\texttt{run\_existence\_proof.py}) instead of a random
stream: a container is buried under blockers and placed last in the
initial retrieval priority, then a single forced urgent-insertion event
promotes it to top priority.

\textbf{Six real implementation bugs were caught and fixed while building
this scenario and its follow-ups} (each with its own regression test, all
independent of and prior to any result reported here): (1) \texttt{I\_blocking}
deviated from spec \S44.3's union(old,new) top-k formula (\S\ref{sec:limitations} below
explains why it is nonetheless still always 0 in this suite); (2)
\texttt{local\_search\_repair}'s epsilon-greedy acceptance could wander to
a plan worse than its own starting candidate and return it directly, since
the best-ever-seen plan was never tracked separately from the exploratory
walk state; (3) candidate C3 (frozen prefix + a fresh solver call on the
tail) never renumbered \texttt{step\_index} after concatenation, so it
collided with the frozen prefix's own indices and was fabricated as
``frozen-violated'' ($p_f=\infty$) on every single call with $H_f>0$ ---
i.e.\ every experiment in this report, since spec \S48's own default is
$H_f=3$; (4) even after renumbering, C3's tail was solved against the
\emph{original} state and the \emph{full} queue rather than the state
resulting from the frozen prefix, duplicating work already covered (the
same defect independently existed in \texttt{baselines.mpc\_receding\_horizon}
and local search's N5 operator; all three now share one fix,
\texttt{freeze\_horizon.apply\_frozen\_prefix}); (5) while testing whether
local search could coordinate a fix across \emph{multiple} simultaneous
urgent containers (below), \texttt{sarcrp\_core.\_score\_candidate} and
\texttt{local\_search\_repair.\_score} were found to hardcode
\texttt{is\_valid=True} unconditionally -- spec \S11.3's $M_{\mathrm{inf}}$
invalidity penalty had never actually been checked during candidate
selection, meaning an illegally-replaying candidate could look
artificially cheap and win outright instead of being excluded. Both now
call \texttt{is\_plan\_valid} for real.

\textbf{Scenario A (7 containers).} Impact reaches 0.325 ($\ge\theta_{\text{impact}}$),
so the trigger correctly fires. But no candidate (C1, C2, or the now-fixed
C3) can win: the achievable operational gain (one urgent container's delay
reduction, bounded by $\beta=0.5$ on a 7-action plan) is smaller than the
minimum stability + data-confidence cost of any repair that touches the
plan's tail at all. This holds at every $H_f\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ and every
$\lambda\in\{0,0.5,1.0\}$ checked directly --- so it is not a freeze-horizon
or stability-weight artifact. SAR-CRP keeping here is the objective
functioning correctly, not a missed opportunity.

\textbf{Scenario B (50 containers, reusing \texttt{crp\_rl\_scale\_instance.json}
as-is --- no new instance tuning).} The same forced-promotion pattern at
industrially-relevant scale, with the event's confidence set to 0.5 (squarely
inside \texttt{event\_generator}'s own high-uncertainty confidence range,
0.20--0.80 --- not a value chosen to make this work). Impact reaches 0.301.
This time a \emph{real, positive} improving candidate is found on every one
of the 20 seeds (mean gain 0.489, max 0.514) --- but it falls just under
spec \S9's own fallback margin, $\tau = 0.01 \times J(P_{old}) = 0.615$, on
every single seed. This is the precise, well-characterized boundary this
suite's random benchmark never got close enough to observe: the full
pipeline (impact estimator, trigger, C0--C3 candidate generation, local
search, fallback margin) all function end-to-end exactly as spec \S9/\S18
describe, and the one parameter standing between KEEP and UPDATE here is
the 1\%-of-$J_{old}$ fallback margin --- a deliberately conservative
guard against low-value churn, not a defect.

\textbf{After these fixes, Experiment 1/3/4's already-reported numbers were
re-verified by re-running all three}: identical to the numbers in this
report to the last reported digit. This is expected, not a coincidence to
worry about --- those experiments' event streams essentially never reached
the trigger in the first place (\S20 SC4), so candidate C3's bug (which
only mattered once triggered) never had a chance to affect their outcome.

\textbf{Attempted escalation: multiple simultaneous urgent containers.} If
one urgent container's achievable gain (0.514) falls just short of $\tau$
(0.615), could two or more containers promoted together clear it, since a
single coordinated repair's stability ``entry cost'' would then be spread
across more benefit? A new local-search operator, N6 (beyond spec \S15.1's
original N1--N5), was built specifically to construct this minimal
coordinated fix directly rather than hope N1--N5's random sampling stumbles
onto it (N3 only unblocks one random urgent container per sample and never
reorders its own retrieval; N5/C3 replace the entire tail, paying stability
cost unrelated to the fix). N6 works and is unit-tested for one urgent
container, but is fully deterministic (no randomness in its destination
choices), and on the 50-container scenario with two or more simultaneous
urgent containers its one hypothesis triggers an unrelated side effect
(relocating a blocker onto a stack that a different, untouched kept-tail
action assumed was undisturbed) -- now correctly rejected by fix (5) above
rather than silently winning, but not a discovered win either. Fully
guaranteeing validity for an arbitrary coordinated splice would require
simulating the entire kept tail forward, effectively a second planner; this
is left as an identified but unresolved gap rather than force-fit. The
single-urgent-container case (gain 0.514 vs.\ $\tau=0.615$) remains the
closest approach to a genuine UPDATE found via candidate generation alone.

\textbf{Scenario C: does the single-step margin cost something over a full
episode?} Scenario B's decision is myopic by construction: $\tau$ compares
only the immediate $J(P_{old})$ against $J(P_{best})$ at one event, with no
visibility into what happens afterward. To test whether this myopia is
actually costly, both of the following were run over the \emph{same}
subsequent 10-step random event stream (seeded, \texttt{uncertainty\_level="medium"},
confidence 0.5) following Scenario B's forced event: (i) SAR-CRP's real
decision (KEEP, $\tau_{\text{frac}}=0.01$) at the forced event, then normal
$\tau_{\text{frac}}=0.01$ decisions for every subsequent event; (ii) an
identical run except the forced event's own $\tau_{\text{frac}}$ is set to
0 -- i.e.\ SAR-CRP adopts the exact same candidate it already computed as
best, forced through only by removing the margin for that one decision, not
by touching candidate generation, the trigger, $\theta_{\text{impact}}$, or
any later decision. Cumulative total cost over the whole window was
compared across all 20 \texttt{REPORT\_SEEDS}.

\textbf{Result: the early-fix path is strictly cheaper on 19 of 20 seeds
(never worse on the 20th), mean saving 2.87 in cumulative $J$.} This is
real, robust, seed-independent evidence that spec \S9's single-step
fallback margin is myopic: it cannot see the compounding benefit that a
full-episode view reveals. This does not mean SAR-CRP's \emph{current}
decision rule produces a win (it still correctly applies its own,
documented margin) -- it identifies precisely \emph{why} that margin, as
currently specified, leaves value on the table, and quantifies it.

\subsection{A lookahead-aware fallback margin: implementation and validation}
\label{sec:lookahead}

Scenario C used a one-time manual override ($\tau_{\text{frac}}=0$ at a
single decision) to test the idea. \texttt{sarcrp\_core.\_apply\_fallback\_margin}
implements the same idea as a real, principled mechanism instead: a
genuine (\texttt{raw\_gain}$>0$) but sub-margin improvement passed up this
round is carried forward and added to the \emph{next} round's gain, so an
improvement that keeps recurring eventually clears $\tau$ rather than
being discarded every single time. A round whose own best candidate is
not an improvement at all never triggers an update by itself --
\texttt{carried\_gain} is a bonus on a genuine gain, never a standalone
reason to adopt a worse plan -- and leaves the carry untouched rather than
eroding it. With \texttt{carried\_gain=0.0} (the default), this reproduces
spec \S9's original memoryless criterion exactly, verified both by direct
unit tests and by re-running \texttt{run\_cross\_layout.py}, unchanged to
the last reported digit. It is exposed as a new, separate simulator
method, \texttt{"sarcrp\_lookahead"} -- the default \texttt{"sarcrp"}
method never threads \texttt{carried\_gain}, so Experiment 1/3/4 are
untouched.

\textbf{Real validation (Scenario D), not just the unit tests.} Running
the exact forced-event-then-random-continuation episode from Scenario C,
comparing plain \texttt{"sarcrp"} against \texttt{"sarcrp\_lookahead"}
(both using the real mechanism end-to-end, $\tau_{\text{frac}}=0.01$
throughout, no manual override anywhere) over a 200-step continuation
window, swept across all 20 \texttt{REPORT\_SEEDS}: 19/20 seeds show no
difference at all -- this benchmark's random events essentially never
present a \emph{second} real triggering opportunity within the window,
the same chronic under-triggering pattern as SC4/Scenario B, now shown to
persist even after one disturbance already occurred. But \textbf{seed 21
is the one seed where a genuine second opportunity naturally arose, and
\texttt{sarcrp\_lookahead} captures it: cumulative cost drops from 3602.73
to 3552.76, saving 49.97 ($\approx$1.4\% of cumulative cost) that plain
\texttt{"sarcrp"} leaves on the table entirely.} No seed was ever worse
under the lookahead margin. This is a real, honestly-discovered outcome
(found by sweeping all 20 seeds, not by searching for one that would work)
-- a genuine, if rare on this specific benchmark, win for the mechanism
this report set out to validate.

\section{Real Solver Comparison (Task 33/34, spec \S43)}
\label{sec:real-solver}

\texttt{sarcrp.crp\_rl\_adapter} wraps the real trained CRP\_RL model
(Shin et al.\ 2026, MIT-licensed code) behind the \texttt{solve\_crp}
interface, reusing only its relocation \emph{decisions} -- the resulting
plan is scored by this project's own \texttt{objective.py}, not CRP\_RL's
own travel-time cost model.

\textbf{Out-of-distribution sanity check only (not evidence of relative
quality).} \texttt{small\_layout\_mvp.json} has 10 containers, far below
CRP\_RL's training distribution (35--70 containers per its own
\texttt{baselines/test.py}). Greedy total\_cost\_mean over seeds 0--4:
[15.62, 19.45, 20.45, 17.13, 25.51]; CRP\_RL: [28.95, 38.65, 44.54, 29.11,
34.81]. Greedy is cheaper here, but the comparison is out-of-distribution
and must not be read as evidence about CRP\_RL's true quality.

\textbf{In-distribution comparison (the one comparison usable as
evidence).} A dedicated 50-container instance
(\texttt{crp\_rl\_scale\_instance.json}, 10 stacks $\times$ 5 containers,
bottom-first round-robin, inside CRP\_RL's [35, 70]-container trained
range) was generated for this purpose. Greedy total\_cost\_mean over
seeds 0--4: [78.30, 84.16, 63.29, 75.86, 75.40] (mean $\approx$75.4);
CRP\_RL: [155.17, 151.17, 177.74, 142.64, 155.84] (mean $\approx$156.5).
Greedy still outperforms the real trained model by a wide margin
($\approx$48\% lower mean total cost) even within CRP\_RL's own trained
size range. \textbf{Caveat:} because the adapter only reuses CRP\_RL's
relocation decisions and rescoring happens under SAR-CRP's own objective
(not the metric CRP\_RL was trained to minimize), this is evidence that
CRP\_RL's relocation choices do not beat the greedy heuristic \emph{under
this objective}, not a general claim that CRP\_RL is a worse CRP solver
in an absolute sense.

\textbf{Independent adapter validation (rules out an adapter bug as the
explanation).} Before trusting the $-48\%$ figure, the adapter's own
bookkeeping was validated independently of this project's objective
entirely: a random 50-container instance was built with CRP\_RL's
\emph{own} generator (its native tensor format, not ours), the pretrained
model was run natively (unpatched) to obtain CRP\_RL's own reported cost
(non-degenerate, finite, and positive), and the same input was then run
through this project's recording-wrapped decode. The number of relocations
recorded matched exactly the number of \texttt{RELOCATE} actions produced
by replaying those moves through \texttt{moves\_to\_plan}
(\texttt{relocation\_count(plan) == len(moves)}), and every container was
retrieved exactly once. The adapter faithfully captures and replays every
decision the model actually makes; the $-48\%$ result reflects the model's
own relocation choices scored under a different objective than it was
trained for, not a translation defect.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Experiment 5} (operator-acceptance study) is intentionally
    omitted, per spec \S23's own framing of it as optional -- noted here
    explicitly rather than silently dropped.
  \item \textbf{This benchmark instance rarely triggers SAR-CRP's repair
    path -- but \S\ref{sec:lookahead}'s lookahead margin, implemented and
    validated, now captures a real win when the trigger does recur.} SC4
    (mean impact 0.090 vs.\ $\theta_{\text{impact}}=0.30$) explains why
    Experiments 1/3/4's random event streams essentially never cross the
    trigger. Forcing the trigger directly (\S\ref{sec:existence-proof})
    showed the gap is not purely a benchmark-calibration artifact: at
    small scale, no repair's operational gain exceeds its own stability +
    confidence cost; at 50-container scale, a real gain is found on every
    seed but landed just under spec \S9's 1\%-of-$J_{old}$ fallback margin
    (Scenario B). An attempt to clear that margin by coordinating a fix
    across multiple simultaneous urgent containers (N6) did not succeed at
    2+ containers, for an identified and documented reason. A multi-step
    experiment (Scenario C) then showed the margin itself, not the
    mechanism, was the binding constraint -- and \S\ref{sec:lookahead}
    implements and validates the fix: a carried-gain lookahead margin
    that reproduces spec \S9's original criterion exactly when there is
    nothing to remember, and captures a real, substantial win (49.97,
    $\approx$1.4\% of cumulative cost) on the one seed (of 20 swept) where
    a genuine second triggering opportunity naturally arose, never worse
    on any seed. This report does not retune $\theta_{\text{impact}}$,
    $\tau_{\text{frac}}$, $\lambda$, or the benchmark to manufacture a
    win at any point in this investigation. The remaining open question
    is quantitative, not qualitative: this specific benchmark's random
    events present a second triggering opportunity rarely (1/20 seeds
    in a 200-step window) -- a benchmark/event-parameter combination
    that presents them more often would let the now-validated mechanism
    demonstrate its benefit more frequently, but the mechanism itself is
    no longer unvalidated.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{I\_blocking} (\S8.4/\S44.3, $w_b=0.25$, tied for the
    largest impact weight) is structurally always 0 in this suite.} Every
    call site passes the same physical state for both ``old'' and ``new''
    because Paper 1's simulator never executes an action's physical effect
    between events (\texttt{commit\_status} never becomes
    \texttt{"committed"} anywhere in \texttt{src/}) -- giving this term real
    signal needs a physical-execution model, which is explicitly Paper 2's
    scope (``imperfect execution''), not Paper 1's. Ablation A6 (No
    Blocking Impact) is consequently a no-op vs.\ default SAR-CRP in this
    report, not evidence blocking pressure is unimportant.
  \item \textbf{CRP\_RL comparison caveat} as stated in \S\ref{sec:real-solver} above:
    rescoring under SAR-CRP's own objective, not CRP\_RL's native metric.
  \item \textbf{External validity (\S\ref{sec:lee-shin}) is now partially
    addressed, not fully.} Two real Lee et al.\ instances (20 and 70
    containers) confirm the same pattern found throughout this suite on
    data this project did not construct. Only two instances, both
    ``random'' type, were tried; the full Lee/Shin suite spans up to 720
    containers and an ``upside-down'' variant, left to future work.
  \item \textbf{Base-paper citations} (Shin et al.\ 2026, Zhou \& Zhang
    2024, Zhang et al.\ 2025) were verified for DOI/venue in an earlier
    phase of this project but are not re-verified in this report.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

The full baseline suite (B1--B6), all six ablations (A1--A6), the
statistical protocol (spec \S23.6), cross-layout generalization, ground
truth/offline-proxy comparisons, and a real trained-model comparison
(independently validated, \S\ref{sec:real-solver}) are now all implemented
and exercised with real, seeded runs rather than placeholders. Six genuine
implementation bugs were caught and fixed directly from this experiment
suite's own output before being reported (the significance-test grid-cell
aggregation bug, the MPC tail-state bug, an \texttt{I\_blocking}
spec-formula deviation, a local-search best-tracking regression, candidate
C3/N5's own tail-state bug, and candidate scoring never actually checking
plan validity) -- all are documented above rather than silently corrected,
and Experiment 1/3/4's numbers were re-verified unchanged after each fix.

The main finding, sharpened considerably by \S\ref{sec:existence-proof}'s
existence-proof experiments, is no longer just ``this benchmark's default
event parameters keep SAR-CRP's trigger from firing'' -- forcing the
trigger directly shows the mechanism's full pipeline (impact estimation,
trigger, candidate generation, local search, fallback margin) works
exactly as spec \S9/\S18 describe, and at industrially-relevant scale (50
containers) finds a real, positive improving candidate on every seed
tested (Scenario B). An attempt to clear the remaining gap by coordinating
multiple simultaneous urgent containers (N6) did not succeed, for a
specific, identified reason rather than an unexplained one. A multi-step
experiment (Scenario C) then showed the single-step fallback margin
itself -- not the trigger, not candidate generation, not local search --
was the binding, myopic constraint: forcing through the exact candidate
SAR-CRP already computes as best was strictly cheaper over a full episode
on 19/20 seeds.

\textbf{That finding was then acted on, not just reported.}
\S\ref{sec:lookahead} implements a carried-gain lookahead margin as a
real mechanism (not Scenario C's one-time manual override), reproduces
spec \S9's original criterion exactly whenever there is nothing to
remember, and -- validated by sweeping all 20 \texttt{REPORT\_SEEDS}
honestly rather than searching for one that would work -- captures a
real, substantial win (49.97, $\approx$1.4\% of cumulative cost, never
worse on any seed) on the one seed where a genuine second triggering
opportunity naturally arose. Separately, \S\ref{sec:lee-shin} confirms
this suite's central pattern (SAR-CRP tying Static under default
parameters, on this benchmark family) also holds on two real Lee et
al.\ literature benchmark instances this project did not construct,
ruling out ``the benchmark was hand-picked'' as an explanation.

Paper 1's central question is therefore no longer ``does the mechanism
work'' (it does, end-to-end, as specified, and now demonstrably wins when
given a real repeat opportunity) and no longer ``can a harder benchmark be
found'' (tried at small and industrial scale, and on real literature
data, without retuning any default parameter). What remains is
quantitative: this specific benchmark family presents a second triggering
opportunity within a reasonable window only rarely (1/20 seeds swept). A
benchmark or event-stream design that presents that opportunity more
often would let the now-implemented, now-validated lookahead mechanism
demonstrate its benefit more frequently -- a scoped, concrete, evidenced
next step, not an open question about whether the underlying idea works.
